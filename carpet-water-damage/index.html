Carpet can often be saved, but the water decides

Wet carpet looks like one problem, yet it is really three layers. There is the carpet face, the pad underneath and the subfloor below that. Each layer holds water differently and each has its own fate.

The biggest factor is the category of the water. Clean water from a supply line or a tub overflow gives carpet a good chance. Water that picked up contamination from a drain, a backup or the outdoors usually means the carpet comes out. Our process follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 reference for water categories. Time matters too, because carpet left wet for days smells and can lose its backing.

A moisture meter and a close look at the water source settle most of these questions.

First-hour safety steps for wet carpet

Wet carpet hides hazards underneath it. Take these steps before anything else.

Do these in order

  1. Stop the water. Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff, then call a plumber if a pipe is leaking.
  2. Check the power. Do not walk on soaked carpet near outlets or cords. Turn off breakers only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  3. Avoid dark or smelly water. Keep children and pets off the carpet if the water came from a drain or outside.
  4. Lift what you can. Move furniture legs off the carpet and put foil or blocks under heavy pieces you leave in place.
  5. Photograph the damage. Take pictures of the wet areas and the source before anything is moved.
  6. Call the 24/7 line. Phone (385) 988-9410 and describe where the water came from.

Do not use a household vacuum

Regular vacuums are not made for water and can shock you. Wait for an extraction unit or a wet vac made for liquids.

How wet carpet is handled

The steps below depend on the water category and what the readings show.

Extraction

Wands and weighted extractors press and pull water out of the carpet face and the pad beneath. Pressure matters, because water left in the pad keeps wicking.

Lifting carpet and pad

The carpet is released from the tack strip and folded back, so the pad can be removed and the subfloor reached. Most soaked pad is replaced.

Floating the carpet

With the pad gone, carpet is lifted onto a layer of air so airflow reaches the underside. This lets clean-water carpet dry without a full tear-out.

Subfloor and tack strip drying

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the floor, and tack strip is checked because it holds water. See structural drying and dehumidification for readings.

Odor, seams and stretching after drying

A sour smell means materials stayed wet too long or the water was dirty. Cleaning and drying help, but some carpet will not recover. We tell you plainly when replacement is the better choice.

Once the floor is dry, carpet is laid back and stretched so it sits flat. Seams can open or ripple when carpet has been lifted, so a stretch and a seam check are part of the finish. A carpet installer is the right trade for large re-stretching or new pad.

Carpet over a basement slab

Concrete holds water and releases it slowly. Carpet on a slab can look dry on top while moisture lingers below, and the pad can trap that moisture against the concrete.

In a basement we usually lift the carpet, check the slab with a meter and dry it before the carpet goes back. If water keeps coming up through the floor, a waterproofing contractor deals with the cause. See water damage cleanup for the cleaning stage.

Do it yourself or call

For a small clean spill, a wet vac, towels and a fan can handle it if you act fast. Check the carpet again the next day for dampness and smell.

If water covered a room, soaked the pad, came from a drain or sat overnight, call (385) 988-9410. The pad and subfloor are where the trouble hides, and a meter shows what a hand cannot.

What affects the cost of carpet water damage work

Scope and price are explained before work starts. These factors shape it.

  • Water category, since contaminated water means removal
  • Square footage of carpet and pad affected
  • Whether the carpet is on wood subfloor or a concrete slab
  • How long it sat wet before extraction
  • Days of drying and the equipment the readings call for

Insurance depends on the cause. Sudden pipe or appliance leaks are treated differently from slow leaks, and flood is usually separate. Photograph first, call your insurer and keep records. We provide documentation but do not promise coverage.
