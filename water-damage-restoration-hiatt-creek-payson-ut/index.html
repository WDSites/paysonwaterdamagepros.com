What the public record says about Hiatt Creek

The Payson City Planning Commission minutes of the May 24, 2023 meeting record a public hearing on Hiatt Creek Plat B. It was a request for preliminary plan approval of a 100-lot attached, townhome-style single-family subdivision at approximately 1130 South 1950 West. The applicant said the open space area is a retention pond designed to hold water and drain during storms.

The commission voted 4 to 2 to recommend City Council approval, and some commissioners raised concerns about density. Real-estate listing sites, which are not a city source, describe Hiatt Creek homes in recent listings as built in 2020 or later with an HOA.

A retention pond is a design feature, not evidence of flooding. FEMA flood-zone status for this neighborhood was not verified, and you can look up a specific address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Where leaks start in a newer townhome-style house

Homes built in the last several years rarely have worn-out pipes, so the usual culprits are smaller. A dishwasher inlet, a washer hose, a refrigerator ice-maker line, a toilet supply or a water heater connection can start a slow drip that soaks drywall and flooring for days.

Attached walls add a wrinkle. Water can travel along a shared wall and show up on a neighbor's side, so tell your HOA or the adjoining owner early. Homes differ in layout, so describe yours on the phone and ask what to check. See also appliance leak damage and kitchen water damage.

How a crew dries a leak in an attached home

These tools do the work after the source is stopped.

Moisture meter and thermal camera

They show how far water traveled behind baseboards and into the shared wall, so only wet material is opened.

Extraction

Standing water on floors is removed first, since the less that evaporates, the less the air has to carry.

Air movers

Fans move air across wet surfaces and wall cavities to pull moisture out of drywall and flooring.

Dehumidifiers

They remove the moisture the air movers release, which matters in a closed-up townhome. See structural drying and dehumidification.

Safety first when a water heater or appliance leaks

Appliances mix water and electricity, so slow down before you reach for a towel.

Do these in order

  1. Look before you step. Keep people and pets out of water that may be electrically live or contaminated.
  2. Shut off water if safe. Use the appliance valve or main shutoff only if it is reachable and you are not standing in water.
  3. Stay off breakers. Do not use breakers or electrical equipment while standing in water.
  4. Call for danger. If you smell gas or see sparks, call the utility or 911.
  5. Photograph. When it is safe, take photos of the appliance, the wet floor and any stained walls.

Sewage means stop

If the water is sewage or contaminated floodwater, stay out. That is a professional job, and fans or household vacuums should not be used.

What shapes the scope and the bill

Price depends on what the crew finds. These factors matter most.

  • Which fixture or appliance leaked
  • How long water ran before it was found
  • Whether the water reached a shared wall
  • How much flooring and drywall is wet
  • How many rooms are involved

Photos can help document visible damage, and moisture readings can support a restoration record. An itemized scope can describe the work. Check your policy and talk to your insurer, because coverage depends on the cause and the policy terms. Your HOA may also have rules.
