Our services

Pick the service that matches your situation

Water losses move through stages, and the right service depends on which stage you are in. The emergency services come first because they stop the damage. The rebuild and mold services come last because they follow it.

Emergency Water Removal

Choose this when water is standing on the floor right now and has to come out. Crews use pumps and extraction units to remove it from basements, finished rooms and crawl spaces before it soaks further into the structure.

Emergency water removal

Water Mitigation

Choose this for the first days of damage control: shutting off the source, starting extraction, setting up drying equipment and mapping moisture through walls and floors. Mitigation is about stopping a loss from growing, and it is the stage that most affects what you can save.

Water mitigation

Water Damage Cleanup

Choose this once the water is out and the mess remains. Cleanup removes wet debris and contaminated materials, then sanitizes and deodorizes the space so it can be dried and rebuilt.

Water damage cleanup

Flood Damage Restoration

Choose this when the water came from outside or from a drain: storm runoff, creek or river overflow, groundwater seepage or a sewer backup. Those sources often mean Category 2 or Category 3 water, and basements get special handling.

Flood damage restoration

Water Damage Repair

Choose this after the structure is dry. Repair rebuilds what had to be removed, including drywall, insulation, flooring, paint and cabinets, so the room is usable again.

Water damage repair

Mold Remediation

Choose this when mold has appeared after a moisture problem. Remediation contains the area and removes the growth following the S520 standard, and the water source must be fixed first.

Mold remediation

Choosing

Which service do I need?

Start with one question: is there still water to remove? If yes, you need removal and mitigation, and those usually happen together. Once the surfaces are no longer wet, cleanup and drying take over, and repair comes after readings show the structure is dry.

Most real jobs use more than one service in order. A burst pipe under a kitchen sink might begin with emergency water removal, move through mitigation while the cabinets and flooring dry, and finish with repair. Sewage or floodwater adds the flood and cleanup pages because the water is contaminated.

Mold is its own decision. It follows moisture, so the leak or humidity source is dealt with before any remediation starts. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, which is why speed on the front end matters so much for what comes later. Health questions about mold belong with a physician.
