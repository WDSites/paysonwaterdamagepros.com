Cleanup begins when the water stops

Emergency water damage cleanup is the work that follows pumping: hauling out soaked debris, sorting what can be dried from what must go, then sanitizing and deodorizing what stays. Call (385) 988-9410 at any hour. The line is answered 24/7, and a same-day response is set up when available.

Until a crew is there, stop the source, shut off the main water valve, switch off power to wet areas only if that is safe, lift valuables and small furniture onto dry surfaces, and keep people and pets out of any contaminated water.

The first 48 hours decide what you keep

EPA guidance says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold. Here is how that window usually plays out.

Order of work in the first two days

  1. Right away. Photograph damage, remove standing water, pull soaked rugs and loose items, and identify the water category.
  2. Same day, when possible. Cut out and bag materials that cannot be saved, such as saturated pad and contaminated drywall.
  3. Day two. Set air movers and dehumidifiers on what remains, take moisture readings and apply antimicrobial treatment to cleaned surfaces.

Do not handle contaminated water bare-handed

Stay out of water near electrical panels or outlets, and call 911 or the utility for any electrical or gas risk before you call us. If the water came from a sewer or drain, or sat for days, treat it as contaminated and do not touch affected materials without gloves and boots.

Category 1, 2 and 3: the label that drives every decision

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard sorts water by how contaminated it is. The category decides whether a porous material gets dried or discarded, so crews name it before any demolition starts.

Category 1, clean water

Comes from a supply line, a washer hose or a water heater tank leak. Most materials can be dried if caught early.

Category 2, gray water

Dishwasher or washer discharge, or clean water that sat too long. Carpet and pad usually go. Hard surfaces can be cleaned.

Category 3, black water

Sewage, rising floodwater, or standing water that has been contaminated. Porous materials such as carpet, pad, insulation and drywall are removed, not dried.

Clean water does not stay clean. A Category 1 leak that sits in warm, wet material can slide toward Category 2, which is one more reason to call early.

Contents pack-out in plain words

Pack-out means moving belongings out of the wet rooms so they can be cleaned and stored dry while the room is opened up and dried. Items are sorted into three piles: clean and return, restore or deep clean, and discard. A written list and photos of each pile help with your insurance claim.

Electronics, books and fabric go first, because they absorb water and humidity fastest. Once the structure is dry, repair and rebuild work can begin and contents come home.

Sanitizing and antimicrobial treatment

After debris is out, surfaces are cleaned and an antimicrobial product is applied according to its label. This step does not replace drying. Mold needs moisture, so an area that stays wet will grow mold again no matter what was sprayed.

If you can already see mold, or wet materials sat for several days, the job may move to a separate scope under the S520 standard. Read about that on the mold remediation page, and ask any company whether its crews work to S500 and S520.

Odor control after a wet loss

Odor comes from wet material that is still there. Pulling soaked pad, insulation and drywall removes the source, and deodorizing products only mask what is left behind. Crews run HEPA air scrubbers and fans while materials dry, then re-check the smell once humidity drops.

A musty smell that returns after cleanup usually means moisture is hiding in a wall cavity or under a floor.

Washer valves, dishwashers and other appliance leaks

Appliance leaks are the quiet culprits. A washer supply valve can weep behind the machine for weeks, swelling the baseboard and lifting flooring before you see a puddle. A condensate line from an air handler can back up and soak a closet. An upstairs toilet can leak into the finished basement ceiling below.

Shut off the valve at the wall, unplug the appliance only if the outlet is dry, and photograph the area. Sudden and accidental discharge is often covered by homeowners policies, while a slow leak over months often is not, so check your policy.

Hardwood floors after a washer valve break

Hardwood cups when the bottom of a board absorbs water faster than the top. Specialty drying mats and air movers pull moisture out through the boards, and meters track progress over several days. Do not sand or refinish until readings are normal and the boards have flattened.

Some floors recover fully and some need replacing, depending on how long they stayed wet. Forcing a floor dry with heat can crack the boards.

What affects the cost of cleanup

Price follows the work, not a flat rate. These drivers are explained before work starts.

  • Water category 1, 2 or 3, which sets how much material has to be removed
  • The affected area and how many rooms are wet
  • Materials involved, from carpet and pad to drywall, insulation and hardwood
  • Drying days and the equipment on site
  • The amount of rebuild once cleanup is done

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners insurance, and gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Check your policy. We help document the loss with photos, moisture readings and itemized notes.
