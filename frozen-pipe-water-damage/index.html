Why a frozen pipe floods after the cold breaks

Ice by itself rarely soaks a house. When water freezes inside a pipe it expands, and the pressure often splits the pipe wall or pops a joint. The split can sit closed and dry for days because the ice plugs it.

The flood starts when the temperature rises. The ice melts, the plug lets go, and a line under pressure pours into a wall, ceiling or floor. That makes a frozen pipe a delayed water loss. If water is already moving, our emergency water removal crew can start extracting while your plumber works on the pipe.

First-hour safety steps for a frozen or thawing pipe

Most of the damage is decided right after the pipe starts to thaw. Stop at any step that feels unsafe.

Do these in order

  1. Shut the main first. Close the main water valve before you try to thaw anything, so a hidden split cannot flow when the ice melts.
  2. Open a faucet. Open the tap closest to the frozen section so melting water and pressure have somewhere to go.
  3. Thaw gently. Use warm air from a hair dryer or simply heat the room. Never put a flame, torch or propane heater near a pipe.
  4. Look before you warm it. Check the pipe and the wall around it for bulges, cracks or damp spots. If you find any, stop and call a plumber.
  5. Call the right people. A plumber repairs or replaces the pipe. Call us at (385) 988-9410 once water has escaped into the structure.

No open flame on a pipe

A torch can ignite framing or insulation inside a wall and can overheat a pipe until it fails.

The places pipes freeze first

Pipes inside exterior walls sit between the warm room and the cold sheathing. Sink supply lines on an outside wall are the classic example. Garages are a second trouble spot, because a laundry hookup or utility sink there may feed from a line that never sees much heat.

Unheated crawl spaces and attic runs follow the same logic. A line lying over a vent opening or through bare attic space can freeze in a hard cold snap, and the split often shows later as a ceiling stain. Standing water under a house is covered on our crawl space water damage page.

Hose bibs and irrigation lines

A hose left attached through winter traps water in the spigot, and the freeze can crack the valve body inside the wall. You often learn about it in spring, when water sprays into the cavity. Disconnect hoses and drain outdoor spigots before the first hard freeze.

Irrigation lines are a separate system. The city's pressurized irrigation feeds yard sprinklers, not your kitchen tap, so a split there floods a yard, window well or foundation edge. Backflow assemblies and valves near the house still need draining.

How we dry a home after a pipe thaws

Our process follows ANSI/IICRC S500, a published reference for water damage restoration work. It is a reference we work from, not a credential.

Extraction units and wet vacs

Portable extraction units and wet vacs lift standing water from floors and carpet before it soaks into the subfloor.

Moisture meters and thermal camera

Meters and a thermal camera show wet drywall, insulation and framing behind finished surfaces. That mapping tells us what to open and what to leave alone.

Air movers and injection drying

Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces. Drying mats or injection ports can draw moisture from wall cavities where conditions allow, which can limit tear-out.

LGR dehumidifiers

Low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air so water leaving the materials has somewhere to go. We check readings each visit.

Thaw it yourself or call someone

Warming an exposed pipe with a hair dryer is reasonable when the pipe is easy to reach, the main is shut and nothing is leaking. It is not reasonable for a pipe inside a wall, a pipe that looks cracked, or any line you cannot see.

Repairing the pipe is plumber work, and we do not do it. Our part starts when water has escaped. If you see wet drywall or a damp floor, call (385) 988-9410. Our water mitigation page explains the stop-and-dry stage.

What affects the cost of frozen pipe water damage cleanup

Scope and price are explained before work starts. These factors move the job up or down.

  • How long water ran after the thaw before the main was closed
  • How many rooms and floor levels the water reached
  • Whether water went into wall cavities, ceilings or insulation
  • Which materials are wet, such as carpet, hardwood, drywall or plaster
  • How many days of drying equipment and monitoring the structure needs

Insurers often treat a sudden pipe failure differently from slow seepage, and a freeze split is often the sudden kind. Flood policies generally address rising surface water, not plumbing. Photograph everything, call your insurer early and keep the plumber's invoice. We provide drying logs and photos, but coverage is your insurer's decision.

Prevention before winter arrives

A little fall work beats a wet wall in January.

  • Disconnect hoses and drain outdoor spigots before the first hard freeze
  • Find your main water shutoff and make sure it turns
  • Insulate supply lines in the garage, crawl space, attic and exterior walls
  • On very cold nights, open cabinet doors on outside walls and let a faucet drip
