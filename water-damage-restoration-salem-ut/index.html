A pond, a canal and a fast-growing town

Salem began around springs that settlers dammed in 1851, which is why early residents called it Pond Town. In 1869 the Salem Canal was completed, about 8 miles long, carrying water from the Spanish Fork River. Irrigation has shaped the town ever since, so a yard that is watered from a ditch or pressurized line can leak toward a foundation in summer.

If you see water along a wall in July and nothing has broken indoors, look outside first. Overwatered beds and a grade that slopes toward the house are common causes, and they are fixed by the landscaper, not the restoration crew. We handle what comes inside.

Why newer Salem houses have their own water problems

Salem had 9,298 residents at the 2020 Census and a Census estimate of 12,234 in 2025, about 32 percent growth. Fast growth means many homes are recent, and recent homes usually have finished basements, window wells and a sump pit. Those features make a house comfortable, but they give water more places to collect and more finished surfaces to soak.

A basement with carpet, drywall and a built-in bathroom can turn a small leak into a larger job quickly. Hidden water in the wall cavity behind a finished wall is the typical surprise.

Sump pumps fail when the weather is worst

A pump that runs for days in wet weather can burn out, and a power outage stops it entirely. Test the pump before the wet season, and know where the discharge line ends.

Window wells collect more than rain

Leaves, snow and a missing cover send water against the glass, then down the frame and into the finished room below. Clearing them is simple. Replacing a soaked wall is not.

Before we arrive in Salem: if water is still spreading

Five quick actions protect the house while you wait.

1

Shut off the source

Close the main water valve if a pipe is feeding the water. If the sump pump is overwhelmed, do not enter standing water.

2

Power down carefully

Switch off breakers to wet areas only if the panel is dry and reachable. Call 911 for electrical, gas or fire risk.

3

Move valuables up

Raise furniture legs, lift electronics, and take rugs and boxes out of the wet zone.

4

Keep everyone clear

If the water came from a drain or toilet, keep children and pets out of the room.

5

Call and document

Salem services, in the order homeowners need them

Most calls begin with water damage cleanup: removing wet carpet and pad, cutting out soaked drywall, and sanitizing surfaces. The longer wet materials sit, the more of the finished space has to come out.

Next comes water mitigation, which means controlling the loss: drying the structure with air movers and LGR or desiccant dehumidifiers, and logging moisture readings each day. When standing water is deep, emergency water removal pulls it first. Afterward, water damage repair rebuilds the walls and floors that could not be saved.

What shapes the price of a Salem project

The final number depends on a handful of drivers:

  • Water category (1, 2 or 3)
  • Square footage of finished space affected
  • Materials: carpet, laminate, drywall, cabinetry
  • Number of drying days and equipment count
  • Rebuild scope once the structure is dry

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not, flood damage is usually excluded, and sewer or drain backup often needs an endorsement. Check your policy. We document photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope for your claim.
