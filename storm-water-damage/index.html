How storms get water inside

Storm water damage starts outside and ends in your living space. Hail or wind can lift shingles and open the roof, heavy rain can overrun gutters and downspouts, and fast snowmelt can saturate soil until it pushes into window wells and the basement. The water that comes in is rarely clean, because it has crossed roofs, yards and gutters first.

Treat storm water as a category 2 problem at the least, meaning it may carry contaminants. That affects what can be dried in place and what has to come out. Our job starts once the water is inside, while a roofer handles the opening above.

First-hour safety steps after a storm

Storm damage often comes with weather that is still active. Put safety ahead of salvage.

Do these in order

  1. Avoid downed lines. Stay far from any fallen power line, and call 911 or the utility. Never assume a line is dead.
  2. Watch the electrical panel. If water is near outlets, fixtures or the panel, keep out and have an electrician check it. Switch off power only if you can do so dry.
  3. Stay off the roof. Wet shingles and wind make roof work dangerous. Call a roofer to tarp or repair the opening.
  4. Contain what you can. Place buckets under drips, move furniture and lift small items off wet floors.
  5. Keep clear of the water itself. Storm water can carry dirt and debris. Wear boots and gloves if you must enter, and keep children and pets out.
  6. Photograph and call. Take photos of the roof if visible from the ground, the ceilings and floors, then call us.

Do not wait for the rain to stop

If water is actively entering and the panel is dry and safe, call us now. Extraction can begin while a roofer arranges the roof.

What we do inside once the weather lets us

Storm water follows gravity, so we start at the lowest wet point and work outward.

Extraction

Extraction units, submersible pumps and wet vacs remove standing water from floors, carpet and basement rooms.

Moisture mapping

Moisture meters and a thermal camera find wet ceilings, wall cavities and insulation that are not visible.

Controlled removal

Soaked insulation, pad and damaged board are cut out. Because storm water may be contaminated, cleaned surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment.

Drying equipment

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference points. See flood damage restoration for larger events.

Which trade fixes what

We are not roofers, so we do not tarp, patch or replace roofing. Call a roofer for hail or wind openings, and a gutter or drainage contractor for clogged downspouts and grading. If the issue began at the roof, our roof leak water damage page explains the inside work.

Our process follows ANSI/IICRC S500 as a published reference. We explain scope and price before work starts, and we keep records so your insurer sees what was done.

What affects the cost of storm water cleanup

Scope and price are explained before work starts. These points shape the job.

  • How much water entered and how many rooms are wet
  • Whether the water came through the roof or from the ground
  • How much insulation, ceiling and flooring must be removed
  • Whether contamination calls for added cleaning steps
  • How many days drying equipment must run

Insurance depends on how the water arrived. Wind-driven rain through a storm-opened roof is often handled under a homeowner policy, while rising water or groundwater is usually a flood claim. Flood insurance typically takes 30 days from purchase to start, so buying after a storm is too late. Document the damage, call your insurer, and we provide notes and readings. We cannot promise coverage.

Preparing for the next storm

Clean gutters in fall, extend downspouts away from the foundation and clear window wells before spring melt. Inspect the roof after hail or heavy wind.

Keep photographs of your home in good condition, since before-and-after images help a claim. We also serve Salem homes dealing with the same weather.
