Local to Payson

Why water damage in Payson has its own pattern

Peteetneet Creek comes down Payson Canyon into town, and the canyon decides a lot about spring. Payson City's 2023 flood-preparation letter reported that crews cleaned the Dry Creek Channel, Peteetneet Creek and the debris basins, and that the Payson Canyon SNOTEL site showed snowpack at 253 percent of normal in April.

The letter said high water generally peaks from mid-May to mid-June. If your home sits near a channel or at the bottom of a slope, that window is when a damp basement can turn into standing water.

Flood insurance matters here for a practical reason. The same city letter noted that a flood policy takes 30 days from purchase to become active, so the time to ask your agent about coverage is well before runoff season, not during it.

Irrigation season and plumbing

Payson runs a separate pressurized irrigation (PI) system, about 5,000 connections, that operates roughly from spring to fall. A broken PI line or a failed sprinkler fitting near a foundation can push water against a basement wall, so a wet wall in summer is worth tracing to the yard before assuming the plumbing inside is at fault.

Older homes and newer ones

Census ACS data for 2019 to 2023 counts 6,941 housing units in Payson with a median build year of 1994. About 36 percent were built before 1980, and about 10 percent date to 1939 or earlier. Older supply lines, galvanized pipe and original water heaters tend to fail differently than the newer homes built from 2000 on, so tell the dispatcher roughly when your home was built.

Snow, water and what it means for your basement

Nearby NOAA stations average about 66 inches of snow a year at Spanish Fork Power House and 55 inches at the Santaquin Chlorinator station. That much snow melting against a foundation is why window wells and downspouts deserve a look each spring. Payson Lakes, in the mountains above town, is a recreation area and not a flood source for your street.

Know your water

Water categories: what the label means for your home

The S500 standard sorts water into three categories by how contaminated it is. The category drives how much can be saved and how the work is done.

Water categoryWhat it usually meansWhat is done
Category 1 (clean)Supply line, faucet or tub overflow, or rainwater with no contact with waste. It can degrade into Category 2 if left.Fast extraction and drying. Many wet materials can often be dried in place.
Category 2 (gray)Dishwasher or washer discharge, a toilet overflow with urine only, or water that has sat for a while.Extraction, cleaning and sanitizing. Porous materials such as carpet padding are often removed.
Category 3 (black)Sewage, creek or river floodwater, or water carrying soil and waste.Containment, removal of affected porous materials, cleaning and sanitizing. Professional handling is the norm.

The category can change with time and contact, so a crew lead confirms it on site.

Not sure which category you have? Describe the source on the phone and we will help you sort it before anyone arrives.

Insurance and documentation

Helping you document the loss for your claim

Large losses need good records

The Utah Insurance Department's consumer guidance advises documenting damage with photos, keeping a call log and keeping receipts for temporary repairs. We build on that with moisture readings, drying logs and an itemized scope of work.

Coverage varies. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage, and sewer or drain backup often needs an endorsement, so check your policy and ask your insurer.

Cost

What drives the cost of a water damage job

No honest company can quote a number without seeing the damage. These are the factors that move the price most.

Water category. Category 3 water needs more containment and removal than Category 1. Affected area. More square footage and more rooms mean more equipment and labor. Materials involved. Hardwood, carpet padding, drywall and insulation each dry or fail differently. Drying days. The number of air movers and dehumidifiers and how long they run changes the total.

Rebuild scope. Repair work after drying is priced separately from mitigation.
