Mold remediation

Not sure where you fall? That is a normal question, and the next section is built to answer it.

Choosing

Which service do I need?

Start with one question: is there still water to remove? If yes, you need removal and mitigation, and those usually happen together. Once the surfaces are no longer wet, cleanup and drying take over, and repair comes after readings show the structure is dry.

Most real jobs use more than one service in order. A burst pipe under a kitchen sink might begin with emergency water removal, move through mitigation while the cabinets and flooring dry, and finish with repair. Sewage or floodwater adds the flood and cleanup pages because the water is contaminated.

Mold is its own decision. It follows moisture, so the leak or humidity source is dealt with before any remediation starts. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, which is why speed on the front end matters so much for what comes later. Health questions about mold belong with a physician.

Water on the basement floor from a burst pipeEmergency water removalThe water has to come out before it spreads into walls and flooring.
Water is out but walls and floors are still dampWater mitigationDrying equipment and moisture mapping stop hidden damage.
Soaked carpet, wet debris or a bad smell remainsWater damage cleanupContaminated and ruined materials are removed and the space is sanitized.
Creek, storm or groundwater in the basementFlood damage restorationOutside water is often Category 2 or 3 and needs careful handling.
Sewer or drain backupFlood damage restorationSewage is Category 3 and calls for containment and professional cleanup.
Everything is dry, but drywall and floors are damagedWater damage repairRebuild work comes after readings confirm the structure is dry.
Visible mold after a past leakMold remediationRemediation follows the S520 standard once the moisture source is fixed.

If your situation has pieces from several rows, say so on the call. The work is sequenced for you.
