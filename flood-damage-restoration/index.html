When floodwater gets into a Payson home

Flood damage restoration is the removal, cleaning and drying work after outside water enters a building: storm runoff, snowmelt, canal overflow, rising groundwater or a sewer line that backs up. Most of it is Category 2 or Category 3 water, so the job is different from a pipe leak. Call (385) 988-9410. The line is answered 24/7, and a same-day response is set up when available.

Until help arrives, shut off the main water valve if a pipe is involved, switch off power to wet areas only if that is safe, lift valuables out of the water and keep people and pets out of anything contaminated.

Water is coming up through the basement drain

Gurgling floor drains, a toilet that will not flush and brown water rising in a basement are signs of a sewer or drain backup. That is a different job from clean-up after a pipe leak, and it calls for protective gear and disposal of porous materials. The trade name for it is sewage cleanup, and it is a core part of flood damage restoration.

Safety first, then call

Stay out of any water near outlets, cords or an electrical panel. Call 911 or the utility for electrical or gas risk before you call us. Sewage means Category 3 water: keep children, pets and bare skin away from it and do not run the washer or flush fixtures until the line is checked.

Sewage backup cleanup

Sewage is Category 3 water under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Anything porous it touches, including carpet, pad, insulation and lower drywall, is removed and bagged rather than dried. Hard surfaces are cleaned, disinfected and dried. Floor drains, shower pans and the area around the drain are checked for backflow, since water can travel under flooring.

Crews wear protective gear, extract the liquid, remove affected materials and run HEPA air scrubbers while the area dries. Furniture and contents are sorted, and most porous items that touched sewage are discarded. Because the source may be a blocked line, ask a plumber to clear it so water does not return. Scope and price are explained before the work starts.

Sewer or drain backup often needs an endorsement on a homeowners policy, so check yours before you call your insurer. Photos, a call log and receipts for temporary repairs help any claim. Mold can follow if wet material stays in place, which is a topic for the mold remediation scope.

Basement flooding cleanup

A flooded basement is usually a mix: groundwater or runoff seeping through walls and floor, sometimes with a failed sump pump, sometimes with a window well that overflowed. The first jobs are pumping the water out and finding where it entered. Note the water line on walls and take photos, because that line shows how high materials were soaked.

Crews extract with submersible pumps and extraction units, then remove carpet, pad and lower wall materials that soaked up outside water, since that water is Category 2 or 3. Concrete and framing are cleaned and dried with air movers and LGR and desiccant dehumidifiers. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show whether water went behind finished walls. Wet insulation and baseboards are replaced rather than dried.

Do not enter a basement with standing water if you cannot confirm the power is off. Once the water is out, soaked contents and materials move into water damage cleanup, and rebuild planning comes after the structure reads dry.

Active flooding: what to move and what to leave

Move small valuables, papers, electronics and medications upstairs. Leave heavy furniture, appliances and anything in deep water where it is. Take photos before you touch things, and keep a log of who you called and when. Pumping details are on the emergency water removal page.

Do not use a household vacuum on floodwater or walk through it barefoot. If water is rising outside your home, follow local officials and call 911.

What the 2023 runoff season means for your basement

Payson City's flood-preparation letter said crews cleaned the Dry Creek Channel, Peteetneet Creek and the debris basins, and that the Payson Canyon SNOTEL site showed snowpack at 253 percent of normal in April 2023. Check the FEMA flood map and your flood coverage

Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask the Building Division at Payson City Development Services about anything near a channel. Most homeowners policies exclude flood, and FEMA FloodSmart says NFIP flood policies generally take 30 days to take effect. Payson City's flood letter says the same 30 days.

That wait means a policy bought after the creek is already rising will not help. If you want flood coverage, buy it well ahead of the runoff period. The Utah Insurance Department's consumer guidance notes flood is not covered by a standard homeowners policy and drainage backup is a common exclusion, so ask your insurer what yours covers.

Crawl spaces and whole-house flooding

Water in a crawl space from runoff or a broken irrigation line is easy to overlook. Submersible pumps clear it, and insulation, vapor barrier and subfloor are checked. When a whole level floods, several extraction units work at once and the category determines which materials are removed.

Restoration is not waterproofing

We dry and restore after water gets in. We do not stop it from getting in. If your basement floods because of grading, window wells, a cracked wall or a missing sump pump, that is a job for a waterproofing contractor, a landscaper or a plumber.

You can look up a contractor on the state's DOPL licence lookup. Fixing the entry point matters, because a restored basement floods again if the cause remains. The same rule holds for homes in Santaquin and the rest of our service area.

What affects the cost of flood restoration

Price follows the scene, and scope and price are explained before work starts. These are the drivers.

  • Water category 1, 2 or 3, with sewage and floodwater needing the most removal
  • Affected area and depth of water
  • Materials involved, such as carpet, drywall, insulation and flooring
  • Drying days and equipment on site
  • Rebuild scope once the structure is dry
